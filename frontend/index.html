Medicine & Disease Reference

Primary medications, medication class, warnings and supportive-care information from the uploaded CSV

Prototype reference • not a prescribing tool
Condition Primary Medications Medication Class / Action Critical Warnings / Contraindications Supportive Care
Typhoid Azithromycin, Ceftriaxone, Cefixime Antibiotics (Target bacterial cell wall / protein synthesis) Must complete full course to prevent relapse and antibiotic resistance. Overuse leads to multi-drug resistance. Paracetamol for fever, strict fluid replacement, soft/bland diet.
Dengue Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) ONLY Antipyretic and Analgesic (Fever and pain relief) STRICTLY AVOID NSAIDs (Ibuprofen, Aspirin, Diclofenac) due to severe risk of platelet destruction and fatal internal hemorrhage. Aggressive oral/IV hydration (ORS, coconut water), close monitoring of platelet count and hematocrit.
Chikungunya Paracetamol, NSAIDs (Naproxen, Ibuprofen - only after Dengue is ruled out) Analgesics and Non-Steroidal Anti-inflammatory Drugs (For severe debilitating joint pain) Do not administer NSAIDs until Dengue virus infection has been fully excluded to avoid bleeding risks. Rest, heavy fluid intake, physiotherapy for prolonged post-viral arthritis.
Jaundice No specific curative medicine; supportive liver protectants (e.g., Ursodeoxycholic Acid under strict guidance if indicated) Supportive / Symptomatic management AVOID hepatotoxic drugs (including high-dose Paracetamol, alcohol, and heavy traditional self-medication) that strain the liver. High-carbohydrate, ultra-low-fat diet, absolute rest, hydration.
Malaria Artemether + Lumefantrine (ACT), Chloroquine, Primaquine Antimalarials (Clears blood-stage parasites and prevents relapses in liver stage) Primaquine requires testing for G6PD deficiency prior to use due to severe risk of acute hemolytic anemia. Antipyretics for cyclic fever spikes, monitoring for severe anemia or cerebral malaria signs.
Cough Dextromethorphan (Dry cough), Guaifenesin / Ambroxol (Wet cough) Antitussives (Suppress cough center) / Expectorants (Thin and loosen mucus) Do not give dextromethorphan to productive (wet) coughs as it traps secretions. Avoid codeine-based syrups without prescription. Steam inhalation, warm fluids, honey (for adults and children over 1 year).
Cold Cetirizine, Loratadine, Oxymetazoline (nasal spray), Pseudoephedrine Antihistamines (Reduce allergy symptoms/runny nose) / Decongestants (Narrow blood vessels in nasal passages) Nasal decongestant sprays must not be used for more than 3-5 consecutive days to prevent rebound congestion (rhinitis medicamentosa). Saline nasal drops, hydration, rest.
Viral Fever Paracetamol, Ibuprofen Antipyretics / NSAIDs Antibiotics are completely useless against viral infections and cause unnecessary side effects and microbial resistance. Hydration, physical cooling (lukewarm sponging if fever is dangerously high), rest.
